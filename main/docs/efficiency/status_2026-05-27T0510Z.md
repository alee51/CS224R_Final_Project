## Details

### 1. B200 (VRAM: plan on **180 GB** for Modal)

**192 vs 180:** NVIDIA/marketing and many spec sheets list **192 GB** HBM3e per B200. **HGX boards and cloud hosts (including Modal) often expose ~180 GB usable** — [Modal’s Blackwell post](https://modal.com/blog/nvidia-blackwell) mentions “192 GB (with 180 GB varieties).” Supermicro HGX B200 listings use **180 GB per GPU**.

**For this project:** budget VRAM as **180 GB on Modal B200** vs **141 GB H200** (~**+39 GB** headroom, not +51). Google “192 GB” is usually the die/package story; what you actually get on Modal is what matters for OOM math.

**Why consider (time-first, +~25% $/s acceptable):**

| Phase | H200 ~ | B200 realistic ~ | Notes |
|-------|--------|------------------|--------|
| Rollout | 90s | 55s | Bandwidth-bound decode (~8 vs ~4.8 TB/s) |
| HF train (sequential) | 280s | 180–215s | Diminishing; **batched logprobs** raise this |
| **Total step** | 380s | **~245–280s** | **~22–30 h saved / epoch** |

$/epoch can be ~flat or slightly higher; **wall-clock** is the win.

**Bring-up:** vLLM 0.9.x, weight sync, FA2/FA-3 on Blackwell, Modal queue per leg — budget **~1–2 focused days** with AI, not “4 days blind.”

### 2. Batched logprob forwards

Today: loop `_completion_logprobs_hf` with `input_ids` shape `[1, L]` per sequence (~100+ launches/step at `n_kept≈512`).

Target: padded multi-sequence forward per chunk → better GPU use; **FA2/B200 matter more**.

**VRAM:** higher peak per forward than one short seq — **competes** with gc off and high token_budget; **vLLM sleep / B200 / 8-bit Adam** help pay for it.

---

### 3. vLLM sleep — what it’s for

**Problem:** One GPU runs **vLLM** (rollout) then **HF** (train). After rollout, vLLM still **holds tens of GB** (weights + KV) even while idle.

**Sleep:** Put vLLM to sleep during HF train → **release that memory** (~65 GB in probes) so HF can use **gc off**, **higher token_budget**, or **batched** forwards without OOM.

**Wake:** Before next rollout or HF→vLLM weight sync.

**Status:** Implemented (`rollout.py`, ablation env); **prod off** — smokes **crash on wake** (`cumem_allocator`). Fixing wake is prerequisite for prod sleep.

**Not** a speed trick by itself — a **VRAM budget expander** for the train phase.

---

### 4. Gradient checkpointing off vs higher `token_budget`

**Same VRAM envelope during HF train** (after rollout; smaller if sleep/B200).

| Knob | VRAM | Speed effect |
|------|------|--------------|
| **`gradient_checkpointing: true` (now)** | Lower activation memory | **Slower backward** (recompute activations) |
| **`gradient_checkpointing: false`** | **Higher** activation memory | **Faster backward** |
| **Higher `token_budget`** | **Higher** per chunk (more tokens in one graph) | **Fewer chunks** → fewer chunk overheads |

So yes: largely **how to allocate VRAM** for train — plus **batched logprobs** as a third spender.

**Does higher token_budget need a bigger GPU?** Not logically — it needs **headroom**. H200 at `n_kept≈512` is often full; **B200 (~180 GB on Modal)** and/or **sleep** make raises safer. Run a short smoke matrix; best combo may be **gc off + moderate budget bump**, not max both.

---

### 5. Subsample / cap `n_kept`

Random subset of kept **prompts** per step (e.g. 32/64), all 8 rollouts each → ~half sequences → ~half train time.

**Changes training** (not just infra). On table if calendar beats full coverage.

---

### 6. Checkpoint every 25 steps

~5–10 s/step when stable. Do once prod runs are boring.

---

### 7. Fused vs 8-bit AdamW

| | Math | VRAM | Speed |
|---|------|------|-------|
| **Fused AdamW** | **Identical** to AdamW (same update rule, fused CUDA kernel) | Same | Few s/step |
| **8-bit AdamW** | **Not identical** — optimizer state (m, v) stored in **8-bit**; standard approx, small drift possible | **~10 GB less** | Similar |

Neither fixes sequential logprobs. **8-bit** is a VRAM lever for §4; **fused** is free perf when convenient.
